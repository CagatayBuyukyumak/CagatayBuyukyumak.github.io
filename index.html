About Me

I am a senior psychology student at Bilkent University. I am mainly interested in how human memory works, especially false memory, collective memory, and involuntary memory.

Over the past few years, I have spent a lot of time in the lab. At Bilkent’s Real-World Cognition and Multimodal Language labs, I learned how to design experiments and analyze data using R, Python, JASP, and SPSS. Recently, I also completed a summer internship at the Applied Memory Research Lab at Jagiellonian University in Poland. There, I set up Qualtrics experiments, reviewed literature on involuntary memory, and translated psychological scales into Turkish.

Outside of academic research, I worked as a Project Assistant at Eşit Nesiller Derneği for an EU-funded project. This role taught me a lot about project management, budget tracking, and practical data analysis in real-world settings. I have also enjoyed working as a Teaching Assistant for introductory data analysis courses at my university.

Alongside my research work, I completed a clinical internship in the Department of Psychiatry at Hacettepe University Hospital. There I observed clinical interviews in outpatient and inpatient units, watched psychological assessments such as the MMPI, WISC, and neuropsychological batteries being administered, and attended case conferences.

Right now, I am focusing on my senior thesis.
